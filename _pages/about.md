My name is George Brooks and I am currently a research scientist at the University of Wisconsin-Madison.

I am a quantitative ecologist and evolutionary biologist who develops theory to understand how populations, communities, and social-ecological systems respond to environmental change. My research combines mathematical models, long-term ecological data, and field experiments to address questions ranging from species coexistence and life-history evolution to fisheries sustainability and amphibian conservation.

Explore this site to find out more!

## Recent Research Highlights

**Species Coexistence and Community Dynamics**\
[Ecological costs of invasive species management create narrow safe operating spaces for aquatic plant communities](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2026.131012) (Journal of Environmental Management, 2026). Understanding how management actions alter community stability and invasion outcomes.

**Population Persistence Under Environmental Change**\
[Concurrent threats and extinction risk in a long-lived, highly fecund vertebrate with parental care](https://doi.org/10.1002/eap.2946) (Ecological Applications, 2024). A theoretical framework for predicting extinction risk of a giant salamander under interacting stressors.

**Life-History Evolution**\
[Fundamental constraints on vertebrate life history are shaped by aquatic-terrestrial transitions and reproductive mode](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-025-02663-1) (Nature Ecology & Evolution, 2025). Revealing how major evolutionary transitions constrain demographic evolution across vertebrates.

**Social-Ecological Resilience**\
[Gradually, then suddenly: transient dynamics of collapsing fisheries in a warming climate](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2026.111829) (Ecological Modelling, 2026). Exploring how environmental change and dynamic human behavior shape fisheries sustainability.
